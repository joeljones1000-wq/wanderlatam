My first-timer's 10-day route

If you asked me for a first trip with a volcano, cloud forest, wildlife and a beach, and you didn't want to spend it in a car, this is where I'd start. It's free. Take it.

  1. Nights 1 to 3

    La Fortuna, for Arenal

    From San José airport: about 3 hours

    Hanging bridges, La Fortuna Waterfall, hot springs, and mornings when you've the best chance of seeing the volcano clear. If your flight lands late in the afternoon, don't drive it that night. Stay near the airport and go in the morning.

  2. Nights 4 and 5

    Monteverde, for the cloud forest

    From La Fortuna: about 3 to 4 hours by road, or about an hour less on the jeep, boat and jeep transfer

    Cooler, cloudier and completely different. Do a guided walk in the reserve one morning, and a night walk if you want to see what comes out after dark. Two nights is right for most people.

  3. Nights 6 to 9

    Manuel Antonio, for wildlife and the beach

    From Monteverde: about 3.5 hours

    Four nights, on purpose. The national park is closed on Tuesdays and tickets are online only, with a daily cap. Four nights means a Tuesday never costs you the park, and you finish the trip slowing down on a beach instead of rushing.

  4. Day 10

    Back to San José airport

    From Manuel Antonio: about 3 hours

    Leave early and give yourself slack. Roadworks and a slow truck on a hill can add an hour without trying.

Why it's built like this

  • Three bases, not five. Every hotel change costs you half a day. Three bases in ten days leaves room to actually be somewhere.
  • Every drive finishes in daylight. It's dark by about six all year. The plan never asks you to start a long drive after lunch.
  • The beach comes last. You end the trip rested, not in a car.

When I'd change it

  • You care more about beaches than forests. Swap Manuel Antonio for Guanacaste, and fly in or out of Liberia instead of San José.
  • You're going in September or October. Look hard at the Caribbean side. It's usually drier than the Pacific then.
  • You've got kids under ten. I'd cut a base, so there are fewer long drives and more pool time.
  • You've only got a week. Pick two of the three, not all three. La Fortuna plus Manuel Antonio, or La Fortuna plus a Guanacaste beach.
  • You've got two weeks. Keep this route and add a fourth base further down the Pacific coast, or cross to the Caribbean side.

Beyond the classic route

Guanacaste
The driest part of the country and the easiest beaches. Fly into Liberia and you're about an hour from Tamarindo.
Puerto Viejo and the Caribbean
A different pace, a different food culture, and often the drier coast in September and October.
Tortuguero
No roads in. You arrive by boat or small plane, which is half the fun, and the canals are best explored with a guide.
Uvita and Dominical
Quieter beaches and waterfalls south of Manuel Antonio, and whale watching in season.
Drake Bay and the Osa Peninsula
The wildest part of the country. Corcovado needs a certified guide, so it takes more planning than anywhere else.

Drive times are typical ones without long stops. Rain, roadworks and traffic can add plenty, so always check on the day.
