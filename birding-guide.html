A taste: the elevation ladder

This is the idea the whole guide is built on. Stay too long on one rung and you'll keep seeing the same birds.

  1. About 1,800 metres and up

    Highlands: San Gerardo de Dota, Cerro de la Muerte

    Resplendent Quetzal, with the best odds in the nesting months, roughly February to May. Fiery-throated Hummingbird. Volcano Junco up near the summit.

  2. About 700 to 1,800 metres

    Middle elevations: Monteverde, the Arenal foothills

    Three-wattled Bellbird, heard long before it's seen in the breeding months. Emerald Toucanet. Quetzals here too, with luck.

  3. Sea level to about 700 metres

    Lowlands: Sarapiquí and Tortuguero, Carara and the Osa

    Great Green Macaw on the Caribbean side, Scarlet Macaw on the Pacific. Keel-billed and Yellow-throated Toucans. The longest lists of the trip.

Why I wrote it

I run a Costa Rica birding group on Facebook with over 2,000 members, and the same questions come up every week. Where's best for quetzals? Is a guide worth it? Which lodge? Why did we see so little on the Pacific side?

Almost every answer comes back to the same three things: elevation, mornings, and booking the right things early enough. So I put them in one place, in the order you need them when you're planning.
