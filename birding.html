You don't get ten days in Costa Rica. You get ten mornings.

The birds are busiest from first light until about nine. A birding trip that spends those hours in the car comes home with half the list it should. I plan yours around the mornings.

Why so many birding trips come home short

  • Wrong elevation, wrong birds. Costa Rica's birds sort themselves by height. Three nights in the lowlands won't get you a quetzal, however early you're up.
  • Long drives in the best light. Changing lodges every other morning quietly costs you your best birding hours.
  • Lodges that look right but aren't. Some feeder-famous lodges are a long way from the trails you actually want.
  • Guides booked too late. The good local guides fill up in peak season.

What's in a birding plan

  • A route that moves through the elevation bands in the right order
  • Lodges chosen for the birding on their doorstep, with booking links
  • Long drives moved to the afternoons, so every morning is spent birding
  • Where a local guide is worth it, and where you can go it alone
  • Your target birds, mapped to where and when you're likeliest to see them
  • The book-ahead list for parks and guides
  • One round of changes
