What birders say

Why so many birding trips come home short

  • Wrong elevation, wrong birds. Costa Rica's birds sort themselves by height. Three nights in the lowlands won't get you a quetzal, however early you're up.
  • Long drives in the best light. Changing lodges every other morning quietly costs you your best birding hours.
  • Lodges that look right but aren't. Some feeder-famous lodges are a long way from the trails you actually want.
  • Guides booked too late. The good local guides fill up in peak season.

Here's what a birding day in your plan looks like

Day 9: San Gerardo de Dota, the quetzal morning

Staying: a lodge in the Savegre valley, about 2,200 metres up, night 2 of 3

  1. 5:30am

    Coffee, then out with a local guide who knows which wild avocado trees the quetzals are feeding in this week. Bring a warm layer. Mornings up here are cold.

  2. 7:30am

    Breakfast at the lodge, watching the feeders. Fiery-throated and Talamanca Hummingbirds are regulars at this height.

  3. 9:00am

    A slow walk along the Savegre River for the highland specialists: Collared Redstart, Flame-colored Tanager and Long-tailed Silky-flycatcher.

  4. 12:30pm

    Lunch, then a proper rest. The cloud usually builds and the birds go quiet.

  5. 3:30pm

    Feeders and the garden trail near the lodge, then an early night.

If it pours: bird the feeders from the veranda. The quetzals are often still about once it eases.

Tomorrow: up early for the páramo near the summit of Cerro de la Muerte, for Volcano Junco and Timberline Wren, back for breakfast.

Questions birders ask

My partner doesn't bird. Will they enjoy it?

Tell me in the form and I'll plan for both of you. Most birding lodges have gardens, pools or hot springs nearby, and I'll pick bases where the non-birder has something good to do while you're out at dawn.

Do you book guides for me?

I recommend the local guides worth having at each stop and give you their details, and you book them direct. The good ones fill up from December to April, so your plan tells you who to contact first.

I'm a photographer. Can you plan for that?

Yes. Tell me what you're after and I'll lean towards lodges with good feeder setups and the spots where the light and access suit photography, not just ticking species.

I don't walk as far as I used to.

Plenty of the best birding in Costa Rica happens from lodge grounds, verandas and short, level trails. Tell me what's comfortable and I'll plan around it.

Can you work from my target list?

Please do. Send me the species you're after, or the gaps from your last trip, and I'll map them to the elevations, places and times you're likeliest to see them.

When's the best time to go?

December to April is drier and the busiest. Quetzals nest roughly February to May, which is when they're easiest to find. May to November is quieter and greener, with more afternoon rain, and mornings are usually still good.

Do you book the lodges?

No. Your plan links every lodge and tells you what to book first, and you book direct. You stay in control of your money and your bookings.
